# Business insights and next investigations

Evidence: [original final report PDF](final-report.pdf), with Executive Summary on page 8 as the primary narrative source. Context is the displayed 2011–2014 full-period baseline in USD; no visible slicer option is selected. Lower executive insights explicitly state that they are unaffected by slicers. Hidden filter state, raw data and original DAX have not been independently validated.

## Technology performance

**Question:** Which category combines meaningful sales contribution with profitability? **Metric:** Executive Summary shows approximately $836K sales, 36.4% of sales and 17.4% margin. Page 2 confirms $836,154.03 sales and $145,454.95 profit. **Finding:** Technology leads category sales and margin in the displayed baseline. **Interpretation:** it is a priority for profitable growth rather than revenue-only expansion. **Next investigation:** compare product-level margin, demand and discount patterns before selecting growth actions. No intervention or causal driver is established.

## Furniture profitability

**Question:** Does high revenue translate into strong profit? **Metric:** approximately $742K sales, 32.3% share and 2.5% margin (page 8); page 2 shows $741,999.80 sales and $18,451.27 profit. **Finding:** Furniture has substantial sales but markedly weaker margin than Technology and Office Supplies. **Interpretation:** sales volume alone is an incomplete performance measure. **Next investigation:** review pricing, discounts, product mix and cost structure; the report does not isolate the cause of the margin gap.

## Regional performance

**Question:** Where does revenue translate into stronger profitability? **Metric:** West approximately $725K sales and 14.9% margin; Central approximately $501K and 7.9% (page 8). Page 4 shows West $725,457.82 sales/$108,418.45 profit and Central $501,239.89/$39,706.36. **Finding:** West leads regional sales and margin; Central has the lowest regional margin. **Interpretation:** Central deserves closer profitability review. **Next investigation:** compare category mix, discounts and state-level outcomes. The executive actions cite Texas; the regional chart displays approximately ($26K) profit. These are associations, not a demonstrated cause.

## Sales growth

**Question:** How did annual revenue momentum change? **Metric:** -2.8% YoY in 2012, +29.3% in 2013 and +20.6% in 2014 (pages 7 and 8). **Finding:** growth rebounded strongly after the 2012 decline and remained positive in 2014, with slower percentage growth than 2013. **Interpretation:** the period supports an improving revenue narrative without implying uniform growth each year. **Next investigation:** assess category, customer and regional contributions alongside profit. Forecast bands are visible, but forecast accuracy and causation are not validated.

## Returns

**Question:** How much sales value is associated with returns, and how do order and line counts differ? **Metric:** approximately $191K returned sales, 8.33% sales share and 14.51% order return rate (page 8). Page 6 shows $191.38K, 727 returned orders, 14.5% rounded rate and 800 returned lines. Office Supplies has 482 returned lines; the reason donut totals 800 lines. **Finding:** returned sales value, returned orders and returned lines are distinct measures. **Interpretation:** a multi-line order can contribute more than one returned line; line distributions cannot be treated as order return rates. **Next investigation:** validate original distinct-count logic, denominators and bidirectional filter context, then compare reasons/products and shipping modes with appropriate baselines. Higher line volume does not establish a higher normalized return risk.

## Shipping operations

**Question:** Which mode dominates order mix, and how long does shipping take? **Metric:** page 5 shows Standard Class at 59.8% order share and 5.01 average shipping days, versus Second Class 3.24, First Class 2.18 and Same Day 0.04. The chart is explicitly labeled line-weighted; the overall displayed average is 3.96. Page 8 says Standard Class leads volume and is slowest. **Finding:** Standard Class is the largest displayed order category and has the longest average duration. **Interpretation:** it is a useful priority for capacity and service-level investigation. **Next investigation:** compare promised versus actual delivery performance and order mix. No causal connection between mode and returns is established.

## Customer segmentation supporting the executive narrative

Page 3 displays Consumer $1,161,401.35 sales, 50.6% sales share and 11.5% margin; Home Office $429,653.15 sales, 18.7% share and 14.0% margin. This supports the page-8 takeaway that Consumer leads revenue while Home Office leads margin. Investigate customer/product mix before making acquisition or pricing decisions.
